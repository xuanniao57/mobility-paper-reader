# Unraveling the spatiotemporal heterogeneity in mechanistic pathways from urban environment to mobility-associated PM2.5 exposure risk

Chenyi Song; Zihao Chen; Wentao Yan · Sustainable Cities and Society · 2025

[来源](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2025.106978)

## 作者摘要

Air pollution exposure has emerged as a critical global environmental health challenge. It is essential to clarify the mechanisms linking urban environments, pollution dynamics, and human mobility. From a perspective of spatial exposure during travel, this study used mobile phone data from Shanghai to dynamically assess residents’ PM2.5 exposure throughout their workday travel trajectories. Three key indicators were introduced to characterize exposure mechanism: PM2.5 concentration (PC), travel aggregation (TA), and travel regularity (TR). By integrating machine learning and causal inference, we revealed the mechanisms and spatiotemporal heterogeneity through which urban environmental factors affect PM2.5 travel exposure risk (PTER), with particular focus on the mediating roles of PC, TA, and TR across different time periods. The results indicated the following: 1) Areas with high building and population density tended to have higher PTER, while the impacts of socioeconomic and meteorological factors are more complex. These variables display clear spatiotemporal heterogeneity and a layered, concentric spatial pattern. 2) PC, TA, and TR show distinct mediating roles. Road density and industrial land affect PTER indirectly via PC, reflecting their influence on pollution emissions. Intersection and Point of Interest densities impact PTER mainly through TR, indicating effects on travel patterns. Various density-related indicators influence PTER via TA, highlighting their role in shaping travel flow and spatial concentration. 3) The road traffic system is a key driver of PTER, with multiple mediating effects through TA, TR, and PC. Its impact varies by road hierarchy and time period. 4) Small blocks and dense road networks enhance walkability but may increase local air pollution exposure, with potential adverse health effects. This study advances the understanding of how the urban environment, air pollution, travel behavior, and exposure interact, offering valuable guidance for spatial planning and environmental policy in global megacities pursuing health-oriented air quality goals.
