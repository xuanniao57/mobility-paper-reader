# Beyond urban form: Introducing dynamic factors in agent-based simulations of pedestrian movement

Marcin Wozniak; Gabriele Filomena · Computers, Environment and Urban Systems · 2026

[来源](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2026.102439)

## 作者摘要

While research on route choice behaviour in cities has focused extensively on the role of urban form, including street configuration and meaningful urban elements, in shaping pedestrians’ decisions, less attention has been given to the role of dynamic factors. These are phenomena arising from social (e.g. crowding and noise) and natural environments (e.g. weather conditions) that vary across space and time. Agent-Based Models (ABMs) of pedestrian movement, simulation tools that allow researchers to observe the emergence of macro-level patterns (e.g. pedestrian flows) from micro-level mechanisms, have been widely influenced by research on static urban elements. However, dynamic factors remain underrepresented in such modelling frameworks. The aim of this paper is to incorporate the effects of crowding and noise on route choice behaviour into an empirically grounded and evaluated ABM of pedestrian movement. In the ABM, route choice behaviour is conceptualised as emerging from the interplay between individual preferences and environmental attractors and repellers previously identified in empirical work. Poznań (Poland) was selected as the study area. First, we conducted a Sensitivity Analysis (SA) to assess the impact of specific model inputs on both micro-level (characteristics of individual routes) and macro-level (agent volumes across the street network) outputs. Then, based on individual behavioural differences identified in the empirical study and the results of the SA, we parametrised the ABM and derived five agent types, each characterised by a distinct set of preferences. Lastly, we assessed the incorporation of crowding and noise into the agent behavioural mechanisms to evaluate the extent to which these dynamic factors shaped the ABM results. The analysis revealed that both urban form and dynamic factors influence pedestrian route choice in complex, non-linear ways, with several interaction effects. Although urban form played an important role in shaping pedestrian routes, dynamic factors also affected route choice: they reduced route length and spatial dispersion, leading agents to concentrate across a narrower set of street segments. Furthermore, we found significant differences in the movement patterns generated by the different agent types. Finally, we observed that agents often traversed green spaces, squares, and other relatively open routes to alleviate the sense of overcrowding in busier parts of the network.
